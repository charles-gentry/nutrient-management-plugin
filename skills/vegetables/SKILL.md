# RB209 Section 6: Vegetables and Bulbs

Use this skill for nutrient recommendations for all vegetable and bulb crops.
Always consult the **nutrient-principles** skill for soil index classification, SNS lookup, and conversion factors.

---

## SNS Assessment for Vegetables

The standard Field Assessment Method applies to vegetables using arable crop SNS tables (see nutrient-principles skill).

### Previous Crop Classification for SNS

Classify the previous vegetable crop by residue nitrogen content:

**High-N residue crops** (use "Vegetables — high N" row in SNS tables):
- Brassicas (Brussels sprouts, cabbage, cauliflower, calabrese, kale)
- Legumes (peas and beans — high soil N after harvest)

**Medium-N residue crops**:
- Lettuce, leeks

**Low-N residue crops** (use standard arable crop rows):
- Carrots, parsnips, onions, radishes, courgettes, fennel, celery

## Sulphur Recommendations

**Brassicas**: 50–75 kg SO₃/ha (apply in seedbed; all brassicas are sulphur-responsive).
**Other vegetables on risk soils**: 25–50 kg SO₃/ha.

## Micronutrient Guidance

### Boron (B)
- Particularly important for brassicas and root vegetables
- Deficiency causes hollow heart (swede/turnip), browning (celery), tip-burn (cauliflower)
- Apply 1.0–2.0 kg B/ha as foliar spray or 2–3 kg B/ha in seedbed on deficiency-risk soils

### Manganese (Mn)
- Risk on light, alkaline soils
- Apply foliar Mn (2–3 kg Mn/ha) if deficiency suspected; consult tissue analysis

### Molybdenum (Mo)
- Critical for brassicas — deficiency causes "whiptail" on cauliflower
- Risk on acid soils (pH <6.0); apply 70–100 g Mo/ha as foliar spray
- Liming to raise pH >6.5 is the best long-term solution

### Iron (Fe)
- Mainly affects crops on very alkaline or waterlogged soils
- Foliar iron chelate (EDTA) as rescue treatment; address underlying soil drainage/pH

## Key Application Guidance

1. **Split nitrogen**: Apply in 2–3 splits for brassicas; first split at transplanting to aid establishment
2. **Avoid late N on bulb onions**: Delays maturity and reduces storage life
3. **Soil pH for brassicas**: Target 6.5–7.0; club root risk increases below pH 6.5; consider liming before brassica crops
4. **Organic materials**: High-nutrient crops (brassicas, celery) respond well to organic manures; always deduct available nutrients from fertiliser programme
5. **Rapid-cropping vegetables** (salads, radishes): Nutrient demand is high relative to growing season; ensure nutrients are plant-available from the start
