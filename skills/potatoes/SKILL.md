# RB209 Section 5: Potatoes

Use this skill for nutrient recommendations for all potato production systems.
Always consult the **nutrient-principles** skill for soil index classification, SNS lookup, and conversion factors.

---

## SNS Assessment for Potatoes

Use the standard **Field Assessment Method** from the nutrient-principles skill, with the arable crop SNS lookup tables.

Apply the appropriate rainfall category (low/moderate/high) and use the soil category from hand-texturing or lab analysis.

**Important note for potatoes**: Organic soils (categories 6–7) and soils following long-term grass leys will have elevated SNS; account for this carefully.

---

## Variety Determinacy Groups

Nitrogen recommendations vary by variety group. Classify your variety before looking up N rates:

### Group 1 — Most Determinate (shortest growing season)
Early maturing; best for early/new potatoes.
Examples: Charlotte, Marfona, Rocket, Maris Peer, Nicola

### Group 2
Mid-early maturing.
Examples: Desiree, Estima, Maris Piper, Rooster, Wilja

### Group 3
Mid-season to late-maturing.
Examples: King Edward, Cara, Sante, Sarpo Mira

### Group 4 — Most Indeterminate (longest growing season)
Full-season; highest N demand; includes processing/crisping varieties.
Examples: Russet Burbank, Innovator, Ranger Russet

## Nitrogen Recommendations

All N recommendations in kg N/ha.
Apply **all N before or at planting** (incorporate or place). Split application may be beneficial on **light sandy soils** (2 splits: at planting and 3–4 weeks after).

### Short-Season Crops (Groups 1–2, lifted before August)

| SNS Index | Group 1 (kg N/ha) | Group 2 (kg N/ha) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 150 | 180 |
| 1 | 120 | 150 |
| 2 | 90 | 120 |
| 3 | 60 | 90 |
| 4 | 30 | 60 |

### Full-Season Crops (Groups 2–4, lifted from August onwards)

## Phosphate (P₂O₅) Recommendations

Recommendations (kg P₂O₅/ha) by soil P Index:

| P Index | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| 0 | 250 |
| 1 | 200 |
| 2 | 160 |
| 3 | 120 |
| 4+ | 80 |

Apply **before or at planting** (incorporate into seedbed or place in furrow/band).
Banded placement is more efficient, especially at lower soil P indices.

---

## Potash (K₂O) Recommendations

### Standard Soils

Recommendations (kg K₂O/ha) by soil K Index:

| K Index | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| 0 | 350 |
| 1 | 280 |
| 2- | 220 |
| 2+ | 180 |
| 3 | 130 |
| 4+ | 60 |

Apply **before or at planting** (autumn or spring). Avoid late applications which can cause scorch.

### Chloride-Sensitive Varieties (e.g. processing/crisping)

Use **sulphate of potash** (K₂SO₄) rather than muriate (KCl) to avoid elevated reducing sugars which impair processing quality.

---

## Magnesium (MgO) Recommendations

Potatoes have a high magnesium demand and are sensitive to deficiency.

| Mg Index | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| 0 | 200 kg MgO/ha |
| 1 | 150 kg MgO/ha |
| 2 | 75 kg MgO/ha |
| 3+ | 0 kg MgO/ha |

- Apply **kieserite** (water-soluble MgSO₄) in seedbed for reliable uptake.
- **Magnesium limestone** can be used at planting for long-term soil building but is less immediately available.
- Foliar magnesium sulphate (Epsom salts) may be used as a rescue treatment for acute deficiency.

---

## Sulphur Recommendations

Apply 25–50 kg SO₃/ha on **deficiency-risk soils**:
- Sandy/light-textured soils
- High-rainfall areas
- Soils low in organic matter

Apply in the seedbed before or at planting.

## Micronutrient Guidance

### Manganese (Mn)
- Most common potato micronutrient disorder
- Risk: sandy soils, high pH (>7.0), high organic matter
- Symptoms: inter-veinal chlorosis; "marssonina" leaf spots in severe cases
- Apply foliar Mn sulphate (1–2 kg Mn/ha) at early foliage development; repeat if symptoms persist

### Boron (B)
- Risk: sandy acid soils; dry conditions
- Symptoms: internal browning, hollow heart
- Apply 0.5–1.0 kg B/ha as foliar spray

### Common Scab Management
- Maintain soil pH **below 7.0** (ideally 5.8–6.5) to reduce common scab risk
- Avoid applying lime within 2–3 years before a potato crop

## Key Application Guidance

1. **All N before/at planting**: Do not apply nitrogen after tuber initiation as it delays maturity and reduces quality
2. **Sandy soils**: Consider 2-split N — at planting and 3–4 weeks later — to reduce leaching risk
3. **Organic materials**: Farmyard manure and slurry provide valuable nutrients; always deduct their available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O before calculating fertiliser requirements
4. **Banded phosphate**: More efficient than broadcast; can reduce rates by 20–30% while maintaining yield
5. **Chloride sensitivity**: Avoid muriate of potash (KCl) for processing/crisping varieties; use sulphate of potash instead
6. **Soil pH**: Target 5.8–6.5; higher pH increases scab risk; lower pH reduces micronutrient availability
