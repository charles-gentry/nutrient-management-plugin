## Soil Index Classification

Soil test results (mg/L) are classified into indices 0–9 for phosphorus (P), potassium (K), and magnesium (Mg).

### Phosphorus (P) Index

| Soil P (mg/L) | Index |
|---|---|
| 0–9 | 0 |
| 10–15 | 1 |
| 16–25 | 2- |
| 26–45 | 2+ |
| 46–70 | 3 |
| 71–100 | 4 |
| >100 | 5+ |

Target Index for arable crops: **Index 2** (maintenance).
Below Index 2: apply extra to build reserves. Above Index 2: reduce or omit applications.

### Potassium (K) Index

| Soil K (mg/L) | Index |
|---|---|
| 0–60 | 0 |
| 61–120 | 1 |
| 121–180 | 2- |
| 181–240 | 2+ |
| 241–400 | 3 |
| >400 | 4+ |

Target Index for most arable crops: **Index 2-**. Grass target: **Index 2-**.

### Magnesium (Mg) Index

| Soil Mg (mg/L) | Index |
|---|---|
| 0–25 | 0 |
| 26–50 | 1 |
| 51–100 | 2 |
| 101–175 | 3 |
| >175 | 4+ |

Target Index: **Index 2**.

## Soil Categories for SNS Assessment

Seven soil categories are used in the Field Assessment Method for SNS:

1. **Shallow soils over chalk or limestone** — well drained, shallow profile
2. **Sand or loamy sand** — light texture, low water-holding capacity
3. **Sandy loam, light silt loam** — moderate texture
4. **Sandy clay loam, clay loam, silt loam** — medium texture
5. **Clay** — heavy texture, high water-holding capacity
6. **Organic soils** — 6–20% organic matter (fen peat/degraded peat)
7. **Peat** — >20% organic matter

Use a flowchart based on soil texture (from hand-texturing or lab analysis) and organic matter content to assign soil category.

## Soil Nitrogen Supply (SNS)

SNS estimates the background nitrogen available to the crop from the soil. SNS Index ranges from 0 (low) to 6 (high).

### Field Assessment Method

Determine SNS Index from lookup tables using:
- **Soil category** (1–7 above)
- **Previous crop** (by residue nitrogen class)
- **Rainfall category**:
  - Low: annual rainfall <600 mm OR excess winter rainfall <150 mm
  - Moderate: annual rainfall 600–700 mm OR excess winter rainfall 150–250 mm
  - High: annual rainfall >700 mm OR excess winter rainfall >250 mm

### Measurement Method

Use when field assessment is uncertain. Requires Soil Mineral Nitrogen (SMN) sampling (0–90 cm in three layers):

**SNS (kg N/ha) = SMN (0–90 cm) + Crop N + Mineralisation adjustment**

- **Crop N**: Measure or estimate N in any overwintered crop (e.g. winter wheat, OSR)
- **Mineralisation adjustment**: Depends on soil organic matter and temperature history
- Convert SNS kg N/ha to SNS Index using the index boundaries (0: <40, 1: 40–60, 2: 61–80, 3: 81–100, 4: 101–120, 5: 121–160, 6: >160 kg N/ha)

## Soil pH and Liming

### Target pH Values

| Soil Type | Arable | Grassland |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral soil | 6.5 | 6.0 |
| Organic soil (>6% OM) | 5.8 | 5.3 |
| Peaty soil (>20% OM) | 5.5 | 5.0 |

For vegetables: pH 6.5–7.0. For potatoes: pH 5.8–6.5 (avoid >7.0 to reduce scab risk).
For fruit: pH 6.5 for most crops; pH 5.5–6.0 for blueberries.

### Liming

Apply ground limestone or equivalent. The amount needed depends on:
1. Current soil pH vs target pH
2. Soil buffer capacity (related to clay content and organic matter)
3. Type of liming material (neutralising value)

Re-test soil pH every 3–5 years. Apply lime well in advance of cropping (ideally 3–6 months).

## Nutrient Conversion Factors

Soil analysis reports typically show elemental forms; RB209 recommendations use oxide forms.

| Conversion | Factor |
|---|---|
| P (elemental) → P₂O₅ | × 2.291 |
| P₂O₅ → P | × 0.436 |
| K (elemental) → K₂O | × 1.205 |
| K₂O → K | × 0.830 |
| Mg (elemental) → MgO | × 1.658 |
| MgO → Mg | × 0.603 |
| S (elemental) → SO₃ | × 2.497 |
| SO₃ → S | × 0.400 |
| Ca (elemental) → CaO | × 1.399 |
| Na (elemental) → Na₂O | × 1.348 |

## General Principles

1. **Always consult soil analysis** — base recommendations on measured soil indices, not estimates
2. **Deduct organic manure nutrients** — account for crop-available N, P₂O₅ and K₂O from all organic inputs (see RB209 Section 2)
3. **Split nitrogen applications** — apply N in 2–3 splits to match crop demand and reduce losses
4. **Sulphur on risk soils** — light-textured soils and high-rainfall areas are most at risk; cereals and oilseeds are most sensitive
5. **Micronutrients** — respond to soil and tissue analysis; foliar application is often most efficient
6. **NVZ constraints** — closed periods and total N limits apply in Nitrate Vulnerable Zones
7. **Farming Rules for Water** — apply fertilisers and organic manures only when soil, weather and crop conditions allow safe application
