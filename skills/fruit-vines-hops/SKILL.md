# RB209 Section 7: Fruit, Vines and Hops

Use this skill for nutrient recommendations for all fruit production sectors.
Always consult the **nutrient-principles** skill for soil index classification, SNS lookup, and conversion factors.

---

## pH Targets for Fruit Crops

Achieving the correct soil pH is the most important pre-planting action:

| Crop | Target pH |
|---|---|
| Most top fruit and soft fruit | 6.5 |
| Blueberries | 5.5–6.0 |
| Strawberries | 6.0–6.5 |
| Hops | 6.0–6.5 |
| Vines (grapes) | 6.0–7.0 |

Adjust pH to target before planting. Apply lime well in advance (at least 6 months if possible).

---

## Pre-Planting Soil Preparation

Before establishing fruit crops, correct soil nutrient status based on soil analysis:

| Nutrient | Index 0 | Index 1 | Index 2 | Index 3+ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P₂O₅ (kg/ha) | 200 | 150 | 100 | 50 |
| K₂O (kg/ha) | 200 | 150 | 100 | 50 |
| MgO (kg/ha) | 150 | 100 | 50 | 0 |

Incorporate organic matter (compost, FYM) at 25–50 t/ha to improve soil structure.
Work P, K and Mg into the root zone by deep cultivation before planting.

## Top Fruit (Apples, Pears, Plums, Cherries)

### Nitrogen for Established Top Fruit

Nitrogen recommendations vary by soil type and grass management:

| Condition | Recommendation (kg N/ha) |
|---|---|
| Bare soil (no grass) | 30–60 |
| Grassed orchard — poor soil | 60–80 |
| Grassed orchard — good soil | 50–70 |
| Sandy soil | 80–100 |

Apply in **late March/early April** as a single or split dressing.
High N rates (>80 kg N/ha) can cause excessive vegetative growth and reduce fruit colour.

### Phosphate and Potash for Top Fruit

Apply based on soil index and leaf analysis results:

| Index | P₂O₅ (kg/ha) | K₂O (kg/ha) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 100 | 150 |
| 1 | 75 | 120 |
| 2 | 50 | 80 |
| 3 | 25 | 40 |
| 4+ | 0 | 0 |

Preferred timing: autumn/winter application allows root uptake before spring growth.

### Leaf Analysis Standards — Top Fruit (July sampling)

| Nutrient | Deficient | Adequate | Excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| N (% DM) | <2.0 | 2.0–2.8 | >3.0 |
| P (% DM) | <0.14 | 0.14–0.25 | >0.35 |
| K (% DM) | <1.0 | 1.0–1.8 | >2.5 |
| Mg (% DM) | <0.20 | 0.20–0.35 | >0.50 |
| Ca (% DM) | <0.8 | 0.8–1.5 | >2.0 |

## Soft Fruits (Blackcurrants, Raspberries, Gooseberries)

### Nitrogen for Soft Fruit

| Crop | Recommendation (kg N/ha) |
|---|---|
| Blackcurrants | 80–120 |
| Raspberries | 60–100 |
| Gooseberries | 50–80 |
| Redcurrants / whitecurrants | 50–70 |

Apply in **late February to early March**. Avoid late applications to prevent soft shoot growth before harvest.

**Note**: "Soft fruit generally has high K demand, especially blackcurrants."

### Phosphate and Potash for Soft Fruit

| Index | P₂O₅ (kg/ha) | K₂O (kg/ha) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 120 | 200 |
| 1 | 90 | 160 |
| 2 | 60 | 120 |
| 3 | 30 | 60 |
| 4+ | 0 | 0 |

## Strawberries

### Open-Ground Production

Nitrogen recommendations by SNS Index:

| SNS Index | N recommendation (kg N/ha) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 125 |
| 1 | 100 |
| 2 | 75 |
| 3 | 50 |
| 4 | 25 |
| 5+ | 0 |

Apply in **split dressings** at planting, early spring and after harvest.

### Substrate / Protected Cropping

Use fertigation with the following target solution parameters:
- EC: **1.2–1.8 mS/cm**
- N: 100–150 mg/L
- K: 150–200 mg/L
- P: 30–50 mg/L
- Ca: 80–120 mg/L
- Mg: 20–40 mg/L
- pH: 5.8–6.2

Adjust concentrations based on growth stage: higher N and K at fruit initiation and swell; reduce toward harvest.

## Grapes and Vines

### Nitrogen for Vines

| Condition | Recommendation (kg N/ha) |
|---|---|
| Young vines (year 1–3) | 30–50 |
| Established vines, low vigour | 30–40 |
| Established vines, moderate vigour | 40–60 |
| Established vines, high vigour | 0–20 (reduce N to control vigour) |

Apply in early spring at bud burst. Avoid high N which causes excessive canopy growth and reduces fruit quality.

### Phosphate and Potash for Vines

| Index | P₂O₅ (kg/ha) | K₂O (kg/ha) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 80 | 100 |
| 1 | 60 | 80 |
| 2 | 40 | 60 |
| 3 | 20 | 30 |
| 4+ | 0 | 0 |

## Hops

Hops have a **high potassium demand** — among the highest of any crop.

### Nitrogen for Hops

| SNS Index | N recommendation (kg N/ha) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 200 |
| 1 | 160 |
| 2 | 120 |
| 3 | 80 |
| 4 | 40 |
| 5+ | 0 |

Apply in **2–3 splits**: first at stringing (April), second at training (May), third (if used) at early lateral development.

### Potash for Hops

| K Index | K₂O recommendation (kg/ha) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 250 |
| 1 | 200 |
| 2- | 150 |
| 2+ | 120 |
| 3 | 70 |
| 4+ | 0 |

Apply in autumn/winter or split between autumn and spring.

## Micronutrient Guidance for Fruit

### Iron (Fe)
- Risk: alkaline soils, calcareous soils, waterlogged soils
- Symptoms: inter-veinal chlorosis on young leaves
- Apply chelated iron (EDTA or EDDHA) as foliar or soil drench; EDDHA preferred on high pH soils

### Manganese (Mn)
- Risk: alkaline or sandy soils
- Apply foliar Mn sulphate (2–3 kg Mn/ha) in spring

### Boron (B)
- Important for fruit set and quality (reduces bitter pit, russeting)
- Apply 0.5–1.0 kg B/ha as foliar spray at pink bud to petal fall

### Calcium (Ca) — Apple Quality
Calcium deficiency causes **bitter pit** in apples. Supplementary foliar calcium applications during fruit development are recommended even at adequate soil Ca levels:
- Apply 6–8 foliar Ca sprays (CaCl₂ or Ca chelate) from petal fall at 10–14 day intervals
- Particularly important for susceptible varieties (Cox, Bramley, Braeburn)

## Key Application Guidance

1. **Soil preparation before planting**: Build P, K, Mg and pH to target levels before establishing perennial crops — much harder to correct after planting
2. **Leaf analysis**: Use annual leaf sampling (July for most crops) to guide nutrient management; supplements soil analysis for perennials
3. **Hops and K**: Always ensure adequate potassium; deficiency severely limits yield and quality
4. **Grapes and N**: Avoid excessive nitrogen — reduces wine quality and promotes disease
5. **Organic matter**: Mulching with compost or straw improves soil structure and nutrient availability, particularly on light soils
6. **Irrigation**: Nutrient uptake in fruit crops is closely linked to water supply; ensure adequate irrigation to optimise fertiliser response
